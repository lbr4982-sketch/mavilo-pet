## Lead Magnet: "First Week with Your Puppy" Checklist (free 1-page PDF)

**Purpose:** Capture emails from new and soon-to-be puppy owners, the highest-intent audience for the 30-Day Puppy Training Plan and the Complete Pet Parent Bundle.

**Format:** One page, US Letter, printable, same design system as paid guides. Delivered instantly by email after signup (Email 1).

**Page content outline:**

- **Before pickup:** Crate set up with bedding; gates in place; food (same as current), bowls, treats; collar/harness with ID tag; leash; enzyme cleaner; vet appointment booked.
- **Day 1:** Potty spot first; small puppy-proofed area to explore; quiet house, limited visitors; crate introduced with treats, door open.
- **Days 2-3:** Consistent schedule for meals, potty trips, naps, and play; potty trips after waking, eating, and playing; start a simple potty log.
- **Days 4-7:** 2-3 minute training sessions (name game, sit); gentle paw, ear, and mouth handling with treats; first short, calm outings; first vet visit.
- **Daily at a glance:** Checkbox row for meals, potty trips, naps, training, play, and handling.
- **Footer:** "Ready for weeks 2-4? The 30-Day Puppy Training Plan picks up where this leaves off." plus disclaimer: "General information only. Ask your veterinarian about vaccines, diet, and health."

**Where to promote it:**

- Homepage newsletter section: "Bringing home a puppy? Get our free First Week checklist."
- Exit-intent or timed popup (desktop and mobile) offering the checklist plus WELCOME10
- End of the potty training and new puppy checklist blog posts
- Pinterest pins linking to a signup landing page
- Instagram and TikTok bio link

**Signup form copy:**

> **Your puppy's first week, sorted.**
> Get our free printable "First Week with Your Puppy" checklist, plus 10% off your first guide.
> [Email address] [Send my checklist]
> No spam. Unsubscribe anytime.

### Email 2: Value and trust (send 2 days after Email 1)

**Subject:** The 3 potty times most new owners miss
**Preview text:** Fewer accidents start with better timing.

Hi {{ first_name }},

If your puppy is having accidents, it usually isn't stubbornness. It's timing.

Most puppies need to go out:

1. **Right after waking up**, including from short naps
2. **Within 10 to 20 minutes after eating or drinking**
3. **During and after play**

Take your puppy straight to the same potty spot at each of these times, wait quietly, and reward the moment they finish, right there outside. That instant reward is what teaches them where potty belongs.

And if you find an accident after the fact? Just clean it with an enzyme cleaner and move on. Scolding afterward doesn't teach your puppy anything useful.
